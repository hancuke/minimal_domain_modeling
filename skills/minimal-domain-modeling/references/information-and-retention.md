# Business Facts and Retention Obligations

Decide which business facts, decisions, commitments, and history must remain
available, to whom, and why.

## Distinguish facts from summaries

Current progress does not replace the decisions behind it. A calculated decision
may need a later explanation. A short-lived reservation may be a binding promise.

Consider facts separately when their obligations differ, even within one concept.

## Identify what must remain known

Identify what happened, was decided, or was promised, and who relies on it.
For each relevant fact or result, ask:

- Must it remain available to continue work, explain history, support audit, or
  meet an external obligation?
- If a past result needs explanation, which facts known then, applicable rules,
  and decision provide enough evidence?
- Would losing the record erase a commitment, decision, or assignment even if
  current progress remains calculable?

State the required obligations. Leave duration and other unconfirmed obligations
open.

## Examples of distinctions

**Good:** Preserve the facts and rules used for an eligibility decision when its
history must be explained. **Avoid:** Recalculate it using today's rules as proof
of yesterday's decision. Current rules may produce a different result.

Other distinctions to consider:

- **Approval progress:** Communication to an external party may itself be a fact
  to retain, separate from individual decisions.
- **Pending work:** Assignment or deadlines may create obligations that a progress
  summary cannot capture.
- **Reservation:** Interruption alone may not end a promise to a customer.

These are questions to investigate, not default domain rules.

## Integrate only relevant decisions

Place retention obligations and supporting evidence requirements with the relevant
responsibility, lifecycle, or invariant. Mark unresolved obligations there.
Report missing evidence or ambiguous obligations as open decisions.
