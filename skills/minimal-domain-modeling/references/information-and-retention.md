# Business Facts and Retention Obligations

Use this reference to decide which business facts, decisions, commitments, and
history must remain available, to whom, and why.

## Distinguish facts from summaries

A summary of current progress is not the same as the decisions that produced it.
A calculated decision may still need to be explained later. A short-lived
reservation may represent a binding promise.

Consider the facts within a concept separately when their obligations differ.
For example, approval progress and individual approval decisions can have
different reasons to remain available.

## Identify what must remain known

For each relevant business fact or result:

1. Establish what happened, was decided, or was promised, and who relies on it.
2. Ask whether continuing the business, explaining history, audit, or an external
   obligation requires it to remain available.
3. If a past result must be explained, identify enough evidence: the facts known
   at the time, the rules that applied, and the decision made, as needed by that
   obligation. Applying today's rules does not explain yesterday's decision.
4. Check whether losing the record would erase a commitment, decision, or
   assignment even if today's progress can still be calculated.
5. State what must remain available and why. Leave the duration and other
   obligations open when the business has not established them.

## Examples of distinctions

- **Approval progress:** Summarizes decisions. If progress was communicated to
  an external party, that communication may itself be a business fact to retain.
- **Pending approval task:** May simply describe unfinished work. Assignment,
  claiming, transfer, or deadlines can introduce independent facts and obligations
  that a progress summary does not capture.
- **Eligibility decision:** May be calculated, but explaining an earlier decision
  requires evidence of the facts and rules applicable then.
- **Reservation:** May be temporary, but an interruption does not by itself end
  a promise made to a customer.

These examples illustrate questions, not default rules for every domain.

## Integrate only relevant decisions

Put the resulting obligations and necessary grounds for explaining history with
the relevant concept's responsibility, lifecycle, or invariant in the model.
Keep retention duration or other obligations visibly undecided where necessary.

Check that required history remains explainable and commitments remain known
after business work is interrupted or completed. Surface missing evidence or
ambiguous obligations as unresolved decisions.
